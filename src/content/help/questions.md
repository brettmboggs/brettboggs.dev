faq:
  - q: "Do I need to be good with computers?"
    a: "No. You need to know your business. Most of the owners I work with would rather be doing the work than learning software, and that's the point of hiring me."
  - q: "What does it cost?"
    a: "A first look is free, twenty minutes. An [operations review](/help/operations-review/) is $350 and credited toward any project. One process automated or rebuilt runs $1,500 to $3,000. A full system that replaces a booking or field-service app runs $5,000 to $12,000. Websites start at $1,500. The [monthly plan](/help/keeping-it-running/) starts at $400. Every build is a fixed quote, agreed before I start."
  - q: "Who owns the software?"
    a: "You do. The code, the data, the domain and every login sit in accounts with your name on them from day one."
  - q: "What happens if you get hit by a bus?"
    a: "Nothing breaks. Everything is in your accounts, and you get a handover document written for whoever comes next. Any competent developer can carry on. [Why it's one person](/help/one-person/)."
  - q: "Do you work outside St. Louis?"
    a: "Yes. I'm in O'Fallon and come to you anywhere around St. Louis and St. Charles County. Anywhere else, we work by phone and video, and it goes just as well."
  - q: "Do I have to stop using my current tools?"
    a: "No. If a tool is worth what you pay, keep it and I'll connect to it. Your current tools keep running until the new setup has proved itself on real jobs."
  - q: "What happens to my data in the apps I cancel?"
    a: "I export it and move it over before you cancel anything. Customers, jobs, invoices and history come with you."
  - q: "Do you use AI?"
    a: "Where it helps, and rarely inside the software I build for you. Most of what a business runs on should do exactly the same thing every time. [Where AI actually helps](/help/ai-for-small-business/)."
  - q: "What kind of businesses do you work with?"
    a: "Small ones, from one person to about fifty. Service businesses, shops, makers, offices. The common thread is an owner who's paying for tools that don't fit and losing hours to work that should be automatic."
  - q: "Can you just fix one thing?"
    a: "Yes. Plenty of work starts with one process, like quote follow-ups or getting online orders into the books. If that's all you need, that's all I'll quote."
