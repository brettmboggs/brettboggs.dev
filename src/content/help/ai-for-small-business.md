I spent ten years running operations: schedules, payroll, customers at the counter, equipment that breaks on the coldest day of the year. Most of what I learned was how to spot where work gets stuck. That's still the job. AI is one of the tools I use to fix it now, and not the most important one.

I use these tools every day in my own work, so I know where they hold up and where they don't. I'll never pitch you AI you can't benefit from.

## Where it usually helps

- **First drafts.** Review replies, Google posts, quote write-ups, the email you've been putting off. You edit; it saves the blank page.
- **Reading messy input.** A photo of a receipt or an invoice, turned into a line in your books.
- **Notes into something useful.** A voice memo from the job site becomes a tidy quote or a work order.
- **Sorting.** An inbox or a pile of leads, put in order so you see what matters first.

## Where it usually doesn't

- Anything that has to be exactly right every time, like totals, schedules and payments. Plain software does that better.
- Talking to your customers as if it were you, without you reading it first.
- Replacing a process that's broken. It will just do the broken thing faster.

## How I'd start

With an [operations review](/help/operations-review/), not a tool. We look at where your week goes. If AI fits somewhere, I set it up and show you or your staff how to use it, in your words, for your business. If it doesn't fit, you'll hear that too.
