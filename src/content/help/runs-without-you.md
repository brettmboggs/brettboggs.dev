In a lot of small businesses the owner still does every quote. The prices come from years of knowing what a job really takes. The best customers get terms nobody wrote down. The logins for the books, the domain and the phone system sit with one person.

That works until you want a week off. It also comes up the first time anyone asks what the business is worth without you in it. A buyer, a lender and a son or daughter thinking about taking over all ask the same question.

## What usually lives only in the owner's head

- **How to price a job.** The numbers you adjust by feel, and why.
- **Which customers get what.** Discounts, payment terms, who needs a call before you show up.
- **The order things happen in.** The steps nobody wrote down because you never had to.
- **The keys.** Logins, suppliers, the person to call when something breaks.

## What I do about it

I start by asking you, and whoever works next to you, to walk me through yesterday morning, one step at a time. The steps people skip because they're used to them are the ones that matter.

Then the work, in the order that pays off first:

- **Write it down where it gets used.** Not a binder. The pricing rules go into the quote screen, the customer notes go on the customer.
- **Build the quote from your own history.** Past jobs and what they actually cost, so someone else can price the next one the way you would and you only check the odd ones.
- **Put the keys in the business's name.** Every account moves to logins the business owns, with a list of what is where.
- **Train the people who'll run it.** In person, on real jobs.
- **A handover document.** Written for whoever comes next, whether that's a manager, a buyer or family.

Some of it is a setting in a tool you already pay for. Some of it is a system I build, which the business owns outright. The [operations review](/help/operations-review/) sorts out which is which before you spend anything else.
