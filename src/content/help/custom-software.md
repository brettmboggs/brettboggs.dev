Most small businesses pay a stack of apps to do one job. A scheduling app, accounting, a website builder, a texting tool, a CRM. Each one is reasonable on its own. Together they cost hundreds a month, they don't talk to each other, and someone spends hours a week copying between them.

I build one system that does the parts of that job you actually use, shaped around how your business works today.

## What it can cover

- Booking and scheduling, from your website or by phone
- Quotes, deposits and invoices
- Customers and their history in one place
- Follow-ups and reminders that send themselves
- The books, or a clean connection to your accountant's
- An app on your phone for when you're on the job

Not all of that, usually. The [operations review](/help/operations-review/) decides what's worth building.

## What you own when it's done

- **The code**, in your account
- **The data**, exportable any time
- **The domain and every login**, in your name
- **A handover document**, for whoever's next
- **New hires** cost nothing. There's no per-seat fee.

A subscription can double next year. Software you own costs what it cost.

## Why this works with one person

There's no company in the middle that needs you to keep renting. I learn your business, build the thing, and stay on to improve it as you change. When something needs adjusting, you call the person who built it. More on that: [why it's one person](/help/one-person/).

[Knock Em' Down Detailing](/work/websites/ked-detailing/) runs on one system I built in place of Housecall Pro and QuickBooks: booking, quotes, invoices, the books, follow-ups, and an iPhone app for the driveway.
