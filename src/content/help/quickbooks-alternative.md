QuickBooks is accounting software, and accounting is a real job. If you have payroll, inventory, several bank accounts and an accountant who lives in it, keep it.

But a lot of small businesses use it for three things: send an invoice, see who's paid, hand a report to the accountant once a year. The calculator on [my homepage](/#overhead) uses $85 a month for that, a typical list price in October 2026.

## Two ways this goes

**You don't need it.** Invoices, payments and a clean ledger live in the same system as your customers and jobs. Your accountant gets the reports they need, exported in the format they ask for. [Knock Em' Down Detailing](/work/websites/ked-detailing/) went this way.

**You need it, but you're typing everything twice.** Keep QuickBooks and stop re-entering. Your booking or sales system sends every invoice and payment into it on its own. That's an [automation](/help/automations/), and usually a smaller job.

Either way, ask your accountant first. I'm happy to talk to them.
