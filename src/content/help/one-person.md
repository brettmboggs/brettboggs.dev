The pitch for most software is that a company will look after things for you. The catch is that the company needs you to keep paying. Prices go up. Features move to a higher tier. Your data sits in their system, in their format. Leaving is made hard on purpose.

I'm not a company, so I don't need any of that.

## What that means for you

- **You own what I build.** There's nobody in the middle to rent it back to you. Code, data, logins and domain are in your name from day one.
- **You talk to the person who built it.** No sales team, no account manager, no ticket number. The person who learned how your business works is the person who answers.
- **It fits you, and keeps fitting.** I build for how your business runs, not for ten thousand customers at once. When you change, it changes.
- **The incentive is honest.** My monthly plan only makes sense if it costs less than what you cancelled. If it stops being worth it, you stop, and everything stays yours.

## Who I am

I spent ten years running operations before I wrote software. A service lane at a Honda dealership, general manager of a car wash, then a group of them, then a district of eight locations and forty-plus people. Then operations for a real estate investment company. Now I'm also the lead engineer on a company-wide platform for a construction company, built end to end.
