One of the most common problems in a small business's setup isn't slow software. It's that nobody at the business can log into something important. The domain was registered by a nephew in 2014. The Google profile belongs to an old employee's Gmail. The website is in the web designer's account, and the web designer stopped answering.

It's fine until it isn't. Then it's a week of phone calls while your site is down.

## 1. Your domain

Look up your domain at [lookup.icann.org](https://lookup.icann.org). It shows which company it's registered with (the registrar). Then make sure you can log into that registrar yourself, and that the account email is one the business controls. Turn on auto-renew. Domains that lapse get bought by other people.

## 2. Your Google Business Profile

In the profile, open the people and access settings. You want the business's own Google account listed as primary owner. Anyone else should be a manager, not an owner.

## 3. Your website

Who pays for the hosting, and whose account is it in? If you can't log in and change a sentence on your own site, you don't control it. Ask for owner access, not just an editor login.

## 4. Your email

If your email is at your own domain, which service runs it, and who's the admin? If a former employee is the only admin, fix that first. Email is how every other password gets reset.

## 5. Everything else

Make one list: every service the business pays for or depends on, the login email, and who has the password. Your scheduling app, your accounting, your payment processor, your social accounts. Keep it in a password manager the business owns, not in someone's head.

## While you're in there

You'll probably find a subscription nobody uses. [Most owners do](/writing/what-your-software-costs/).

## How I handle it

Everything I set up is in accounts with the client's name on them, and they get a handover document listing every login. If they stop working with me, nothing breaks and nothing has to be pried loose. That's [the point of working with one person](/help/one-person/) instead of a platform.
