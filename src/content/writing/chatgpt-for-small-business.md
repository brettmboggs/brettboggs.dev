Plenty of owners have tried ChatGPT or something like it once or twice, got a strange answer, and stopped. Others have been told it will run their business. Neither is right. It's a tool that's very good at a few things.

## Where it helps

**First drafts.** The reply to a bad review. A Google post. The email to a customer who hasn't paid. The job description you've been putting off. It gets you from a blank page to something you can fix in two minutes.

**Rewriting in your voice.** Paste in three emails you've sent that you're happy with and ask it to write the next one like those. It gets much better.

**Reading messy things.** A photo of a receipt, a supplier's PDF price list, a long email chain. Ask for the totals, the dates, or what the customer actually asked for.

**Turning notes into documents.** Talk through a job into your phone on the drive home. Paste the transcript in and ask for a tidy quote or work order.

**Explaining things.** A clause in a lease, a tax notice, an error message. Then check the important part with the person who'd know.

## Where it doesn't

**Anything that has to be exactly right every time.** Totals, schedules, payroll, inventory counts. It can be confidently wrong. Plain software and a spreadsheet do this better.

**Talking to customers as you, unread.** It will invent a policy or a price. Read it before it goes out.

**Fixing a broken process.** If quotes go out late because nobody owns them, AI will help you write a late quote faster.

## Two habits worth having

1. **Give it context.** "Write a review reply" gets you something bland. "Write a short, friendly reply from the owner of a family mattress store to this review, and mention we'll call them" gets you something usable.
2. **Keep customer details out unless you've checked.** Read what the tool keeps and who can see it before you paste anything private in.

## Where to start

Pick the one writing job you dread every week and use it for that, every time, for a month. If it doesn't save you an hour a week, it's not for you yet, and that's fine.
