Spreadsheets run more small businesses than any software company would like to admit. They're free, flexible and everyone knows how to use one. For a long time they're the right answer.

Then one day they aren't, and it usually isn't obvious when that happened.

## The signs

**Two people can't work at once.** Someone has it open, someone else saves over them, and a week of changes disappears.

**There's a "real" copy.** "Schedule FINAL v3 (Mike's)" lives on one laptop and everyone else is guessing.

**The same thing gets typed twice.** A customer goes into the spreadsheet, then the invoice, then the books. Every copy is a chance to get it wrong.

**One person understands it.** The formulas, the colors, the hidden tabs. When they're on vacation, nobody touches it.

**Nothing reminds you.** The sheet knows a quote went out ten days ago. It can't tell you to follow up.

**It doesn't work on a phone.** Your crew or staff need it in the field, and they're squinting at tiny cells or calling the office.

If three or more of those are true, the spreadsheet has become the slowest part of your business.

## What not to do

Don't buy a separate app for each problem. That's how a business ends up with a scheduling app, a CRM, an invoicing tool and a texting platform that don't talk to each other, [and a bill to match](/writing/what-your-software-costs/).

## What to do instead

1. **Write down what the spreadsheet actually does.** Each tab, in one line.
2. **Mark what hurts.** Usually two or three of the signs above, not all of them.
3. **Fix those first.** Sometimes that's a shared online sheet and a form for the field. Sometimes it's a single tool that does the one job well.
4. **If the whole thing needs replacing,** replace it with one system that does what your spreadsheet did, plus the reminders and the phone view, and that you own.

That last step is what [custom software](/help/custom-software/) means for a small business. Often it's a simpler, cheaper thing than it sounds.
