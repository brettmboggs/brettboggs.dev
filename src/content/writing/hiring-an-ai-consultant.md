Search for an AI consultant near you and you'll find a lot of new websites. Some are good. Some are a template with your city's name swapped in, run from another state, selling the same chatbot to everyone. Here's how to tell the difference in one phone call.

## 1. "What would you look at first?"

The right answer is your business: how a job moves from the first call to getting paid, and where it gets stuck. If the first answer is a product, they've decided what to sell you before they've seen anything.

## 2. "Where would you not use AI?"

Someone who knows the tools knows their limits. If everything is a fit, nothing was assessed.

## 3. "What will it cost me every month after you're done?"

Get the number in writing: their fee, the software, the AI usage. Many setups are cheap to build and expensive to keep. Plain automation often costs nothing to run.

## 4. "Who owns it?"

The accounts, the data, the prompts, the code. If you part ways, can you keep using what you paid for? If it all lives in their account, you're renting it from them.

## 5. "Can I see something you've built that's running today?"

Not a slide or a demo. A real business using it. Ask to talk to the owner.

## 6. "What happens when it breaks?"

Who notices, how fast, and what it costs to fix. Anything worth paying for should tell someone when it fails.

## 7. "Who will I actually be working with?"

At a lot of agencies, the person on the sales call isn't the person who builds it. That's not always bad, but you should know.
