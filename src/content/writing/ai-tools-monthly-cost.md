Every app you already pay for is adding an AI feature, and most of them want more money for it. New tools show up every week promising to answer your phones, write your marketing or run your inbox. Some of them are worth it. Many are a subscription in search of a problem.

Ask these before you sign up.

## 1. What job does it do, exactly?

One sentence: "It reads incoming emails and drafts replies to quote requests." If you can't write the sentence, you're buying a demo.

## 2. How often does that job happen?

A tool that saves ten minutes on something you do twice a month isn't saving you anything at $50 a month.

## 3. How is it priced?

Per seat, per message, per minute, per "credit"? Usage pricing looks cheap in the trial and grows with your business. Ask what last month would have cost at your real volume.

## 4. Does it need to be AI at all?

A surprising number of "AI" features are a rule anyone could write. "If a quote has no reply in three days, send a reminder" is not intelligence. It's an automation, and it costs nothing to run once it's built. Save AI for the parts that need judgment on messy input: reading a photo, a voicemail, a rambling email.

## 5. What happens to your data?

Where does it go, who can see it, is it used to train anything, and can you get it back out if you leave? If the answers are vague, so is your control of it.

## 6. What happens when it's wrong?

It will be, sometimes. Does a person see the output before a customer does? Who notices if it stops working?

## The pattern I use

Most of what I build for clients is plain software: forms, rules, reminders and connections between the tools they already have. It does the same thing every time and has no monthly AI bill. AI goes in only where it clearly pays, and when it does, it runs on the client's own account so they see exactly what it costs.
