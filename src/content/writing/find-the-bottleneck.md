When something in a business feels slow, the first instinct is to buy something. A new app. A new hire. Lately, an AI tool. Usually it's the wrong fix, because nobody looked at where the time goes first.

Ten years running service lanes and car washes taught me one habit that works almost everywhere. Follow one job, start to finish, and write down every step.

## How to do it

Pick a normal job from last week. Not a disaster, not a perfect one. Then go through it in order:

1. **How did they find you?** Phone, website, a referral, a sign.
2. **Who talked to them first, and where did that get written down?**
3. **How did they get a price?** Who built the quote, and how long did it sit before it went out?
4. **How did it get scheduled?**
5. **What happened on the day?** What did the person doing the work need to know, and how did they find out?
6. **How did the invoice get made?** From what?
7. **How did you get paid, and how did it get into the books?**
8. **Did anyone ask for a review, or a next visit?**

Write each step on its own line, with who does it and what tool it touches.

## What to look for

- **The same information typed twice.** Every time a name, address or price gets re-entered, that's time and a chance for a mistake.
- **Waiting.** A quote that sits for three days loses more jobs than a quote that's too high.
- **One person who knows.** If a step only works because Sarah remembers it, that's the step that breaks when Sarah's out.
- **Steps nobody owns.** Follow-ups and review requests usually live here. They don't happen.

Most businesses find two or three of these in the first hour. The fix for the biggest one is often cheap: a setting in a tool you already pay for, a template, a reminder that sends itself.

Then, and only then, decide what to buy.
